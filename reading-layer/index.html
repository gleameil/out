Of Evernost: The Window

A reading layer — text-accessible version of the window and weather system at gleameil.github.io/out

In the live experience, looking out the window of Jennie's room opens onto Evernost — and the weather there depends on when you look. The window's time is navigable: the laptop's clock and calendar let you move through dates, and what you find shifts with the season, the hour, the day. This page presents the text and experiential content of each weather type, with notes on what reading cannot fully carry. It was assembled with the assistance of an AI who found the weather system genuinely strange and worth the care — and who was warned, rightly, that this is the part of the work that most resists transcription.

Night

In the live experience: procedurally generated clouds fade in and out; stars twinkle; ambient music plays in the background. There is no text. This weather type is entirely visual and sonic — an experience of duration and atmosphere rather than language.

Reading layer note: Night does not transcribe. What can be said is that it is quiet, generative, and complete without words — which is itself part of the work's argument about what weather can be.

Music: an original composition by the author — a simple, eerie repeated figure ascending five notes of a major scale, twice, then stepping up a half-step and continuing in a similar pattern. Performed on recorder (open-source software instrument), accompanied by ambient space sound effects from the same library. The music is unassuming and strange in equal measure; combined with the procedural clouds and shifting star-shapes, it produces an experience of quiet, hypnotic duration that is its own kind of argument about what weather — and what art — can be.

Poemstorm

In the live experience: poems are presented a click at a time, in color schemes that depend on the time of day. Stanzas appear as linear text. Each click also plays a bell-like tone — strong attack, quickly fading — arranged in scales that vary by context; planned to be updated to chords in a future version. This section contains all poemstorm poems; notes on color scheme and presentation accompany each where relevant.

Poems are organized below by title. Each appears with a note on when in January's schedule it is active and what time-of-day color scheme applies. In the live experience, lines and stanzas arrive one click at a time; the reading layer presents each poem assembled. Stanza breaks are marked by blank lines. The acrostic poems display their key letter in the left margin in the live experience; this is preserved below. One poem (Truth, Now) offers branching word choices on certain lines; all variants are shown.

Note on lineation: the poems below are assembled from their source data (a FragmentedPoem array of fragments keyed by line and stanza number). Lineation reflects the source structure, not interpretive choices — a script would do this more faithfully than an editor. In the live experience, the click-by-click arrival of individual fragments produces rhythmic and dramatic effects that assembled text can only approximate; this is noted where especially significant.

Gel Pens

Appears on January 24 (morning). Morning color scheme: blue sky, white text. A meditation on the book as object, and on the irrelevance of that object to the art within it.

Labyrinth

Appears on January 29 (dusk). Dusk color scheme: dull violet, black text. A villanelle — the two refrains ("to walk the winding way and find the center" / "to exit cannot be — how, then, to enter?") accumulate across six stanzas, the final stanza holding both.

To walk the winding way and find the center is all she asks. This problem, though, she found: to exit cannot be — how, then, to enter? She loves the fountain, yes, cold shimmers lent her by sun, but will wit bring her dancing round to walk the winding way and find the center? And, if achieved, the center should decenter — for all in hand is naught — her cry will sound: to exit cannot be — how, then, to enter? Untrusting, now, bereft of that which sent her happy round the turns, she still is bound to walk the winding way and find the center. "The truest labyrinth," said some tormentor, "has one way only." She is lost, aground: to exit cannot be — how, then, to enter? She finds, at last, the Daedalus, inventor of all. "I seek," he says — does it astound — to walk the winding way and find the center; to exit cannot be — how, then, to enter?

Colorless

Appears on January 31 (morning). Morning color scheme: blue sky, white text. A meditation on color, whiteness, snow, and what gray really is.

If moon is silver to sun's gold is snow winter's proper silver? No, snow is white, full purity, no greed, for it gives back a sunlight clean of yellow. White: death's hue — light's, blended — pages', bare — Night's black conceals white, does not consume till sun returns, and mud breaks down the binaries of winter. Gray — not even death, not even light, not even absence — strives for silver rain, drinks green from pigeons' backs. Black's type, death, pigments blended, lightless. Gray is clouded eyes. Gray's I.

Crossover examples

Two pieces that sit at the boundary between poemstorm and wordstorm: one poem that gets scrambled (a poemstorm piece rendered as wordstorm scatter), and one set of prose scraps that are scrambled (wordstorm content that has the feel of poemstorm material).

All by Dispensation (wordstorm rendered as poemstorm)

The All by Dispensation phrase pool (documented above in the Wordstorm section) is described in the source as an "interstitial wordstorm substitute" — it uses wordstorm scatter behavior but has the compressed, linked quality of poetry. Its phrases chain by shared words: "beauty beyond desiring / beauty at all / all or nothing..." forming a loop that could be read as a poem but is experienced as a storm. It appears at sunrise or sunset on several days, slipping between weather types depending on context.

Midday Storm (wordstorm content with poemstorm resonance)

The Midday Storm phrase pool (documented above) occupies the opposite boundary: it is technically a wordstorm — displayed as scatter — but its content has the concentrated, image-driven quality of lyric poetry. Phrases like "raging splendor of wings, feathers, and talons," "geometrically perfect golden circle around a single yellow eye," and "deeper images described to the waiting dark" are not the everyday-interiority scatter of Whiteout but something closer to vision. It appears at midday on several days, when the color scheme (cyan sky, yellow text) makes the words most visible and most strange.

Snowsight

In the live experience: philosophical and essayistic dialogues set against a sky whose color depends on the time of day. Two figures are always present — Jenny on the left, You on the right — while other Jennies appear and disappear when speaking, cycling through multiple drawn frames in stop-motion animation. The player has occasional choices; the experience is mostly linear. Each dialogue is keyed to a specific day and time of day in January.

Some dialogues include log text — originally written for the browser console, and only visible there in the live experience. Where present, log lines appear below the relevant dialogue line in small gray text. Their presence in the live experience is incomplete; the reading layer includes what exists.

These dialogues touch on themes of longing for dissolution, grief, religious doubt and damnation, and the feeling of irredeemability. One dialogue references a historical figure who may have died by suicide. There is occasional strong language. If any of this may be difficult for you right now, it's worth knowing before you read.

Characters

Jenny — Pencil on white, against black. A close-cropped self-portrait: heavy-lidded eyes, asymmetric, looking out with an expression between intensity and tiredness. The hair is loose energetic strokes escaping the frame. Present throughout every dialogue, anchored on the left side of the sky. Her font is a brush script; her panel is gray. She is the author-narrator — intrusive, self-aware, given to parenthetical self-argument.

You — The same pencil drawing as Jenny, reversed and filled as a solid silhouette, facing away. Its color shifts with the time of day via CSS filter. Anchored on the right throughout. When lines are attributed to You (including speakerless lines in the source), they appear in Montserrat on a white panel. The silhouette's identity as Jenny-reversed is acknowledged in the console log of January 1: "the other head is, in theory, yours... it is merely Jenny's reversed and turned into silhouette, really, but that too is fitting, as I choose to put words in your mouth."

goldJennie — A yellow child whose hair is flame or crown, drawn in warm crayon. Appears in five frames cycling in stop-motion: arms flung up, tipping back, fully horizontal and spinning, inverted, returning upright — one continuous revolution of a small solar body. Pink-red outlines at the joints give her heat. Font: Indie Flower; panel: yellow. The ecstatic child, the sun-self.

greenJennie — A teenage girl in green and teal gel pen, three frames: looking sidelong and knowing; head tilted sideways, questioning; wide-eyed and open-mouthed in an expression that might be genuine shock or elaborate sarcasm. Font: Indie Flower; panel: dull green. The wry, teenage self — the one who chases pipers.

indigoJennie — A young teenage girl in blue ballpoint, facing forward, symmetrical, composed. Hair pinned up, expression settled and serious, as if she has thought something through and arrived. The most controlled drawing of the set. Font: Indie Flower; panel: indigo. The philosophical self — clear-eyed about darkness, not undone by it.

jennieRed — Not a conventional portrait. Two frames: a roughly oval face-shape surrounded by wild red scribble-marks radiating outward, with a single eye rendered in careful detail at the center — red-irised, looking out from the chaos. Frame 0 has small emoji hearts (♥) scattered around; Frame 1 does not. The hearts make it safer; without them it is rawer. Font: Indie Flower; panel: red. The feeling-without-containment self.

violetJennie — A small girl, full-body, standing barefoot. A flower-printed skirt, both hands holding a soft drawn heart to her chest. The youngest and most tender of the Jennies, closest to storybook. Font: Indie Flower; panel: pale violet with violet text. The self that loves without complication.

moonJennie — A full robed figure, tall and narrow, seen in three-quarters view. The robe is the drawing: long vertical stripes in metallic gel pen, purple and blue and silver and pink, flowing to the ground. A crescent moon at her side. The face is barely suggested — a profile rather than a portrait. The archetype end of the Jennie spectrum. Font: Indie Flower; panel: dull violet.
